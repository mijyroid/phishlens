How do I get an email's source? Step-by-step help for Gmail, Outlook, Apple Mail and Yahoo

Every email has hidden details, called its source, that show who really sent it. Here's how to copy them. Pick the email service you use:

  1. Open the email in Gmail on a computer.
  2. Next to the Reply arrow at the top right of the email, click the three dots ⋮.
  3. Click Show original. A new tab opens with a lot of text. That's normal.
  4. Click the blue Copy to clipboard button.
  5. Come back to this page, click in the box above, and paste: press ⌘ + V on a Mac or Ctrl + V on Windows.
  6. Click Analyze email.

Prefer a file? In step 3, click Download message instead. Then open the Upload .eml tab and choose the file you downloaded.

  1. Open the email in Outlook on the web (outlook.com) or the new Outlook app.
  2. Click the three dots … at the top right of the email.
  3. Choose View, then View message source. A window opens with a lot of text.
  4. Click inside that window, select everything with ⌘ + A (Mac) or Ctrl + A (Windows), then copy with ⌘ + C or Ctrl + C.
  5. Come back to this page, click in the box above, and paste with ⌘ + V or Ctrl + V.
  6. Click Analyze email.
  1. Open the email in the Mail app on your Mac.
  2. In the menu bar at the very top of the screen, click View, then Message, then Raw Source.
  3. Select everything with ⌘ + A, then copy with ⌘ + C.
  4. Come back to this page, click in the box above, and paste with ⌘ + V.
  5. Click Analyze email.

Prefer a file? With the email open, choose File → Save As…, set Format to Raw Message Source, and save it. Then choose that file on the Upload .eml tab.

  1. Open the email in Yahoo Mail on a computer.
  2. Click the three dots … (More) in the toolbar above the email.
  3. Click View raw message. A new tab opens with a lot of text.
  4. Select everything with ⌘ + A (Mac) or Ctrl + A (Windows), then copy with ⌘ + C or Ctrl + C.
  5. Come back to this page, click in the box above, and paste with ⌘ + V or Ctrl + V.
  6. Click Analyze email.

On a phone? Phone email apps usually can't show the source. Use a computer, or ask someone you trust to check the email for you.

Still stuck? You can paste just the words of the email instead. PhishLens will still check the links and wording, but it can't tell who really sent it.

While you check: don't click any links or open any attachments in the email.
